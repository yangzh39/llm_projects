SPECIAL TEAMS POLICY NOTES (Training / RAG demo)

1) What counts as special teams
- Kickoffs and kickoff returns
- Punts and punt returns
- Field goal and extra point attempts
- Onside kick attempts
- Fake punt/field goal concepts (still “special teams plays”)

2) Kickoffs (high-level)
- Kickoff starts a new possession sequence. Receiving team may return the kick or take a touchback.
- Touchback concept: ball is brought out to a standard spot when the kick ends in the end zone or is downed according to rules.

3) Punts (high-level)
- Punt is a strategic kick on typically 4th down to transfer possession and improve field position.
- If the punt is caught and returned, live play continues until the returner is tackled/out of bounds.
- If the punt is downed without a return, possession changes at the dead-ball spot.
- Touchback concept also applies when the punt ends in the end zone (ball comes out to standard spot).

4) Field goals and extra points
- Field goal is worth 3 points if the kicked ball passes through uprights and above the crossbar.
- Extra point is a placekick after a touchdown (worth 1 point).
- Two-point try is an offensive play, but it occurs in the post-touchdown phase (not a kick).

5) Common special teams penalties (conceptual)
- Offside/illegal formation on kick plays can negate returns or create re-kicks.
- Illegal block in the back or holding can bring back a big return and change field position.
- Kick-catch interference / fair catch interference (conceptual): interfering with a returner’s right to catch a kick.

6) Fake plays and risk management
- Fake punt/FG uses deception; it can extend a drive but risks giving opponent short field position.
- Onside kick attempts aim to recover the kick; high variance play used when trailing late.

End of special teams notes.
