OFFENSE POLICY NOTES (Training / RAG demo)

1) Ball possession and downs
- The offense gets four downs to gain 10 yards. If they gain 10+ yards, they earn a new set of downs (“first down”).
- If the offense fails to gain 10 yards after 4 downs, possession typically changes (turnover on downs) unless a punt/field goal attempt occurs on 4th down.

2) Scoring options
- Touchdown: scoring play worth 6 points when the ball is possessed in or crosses into the opponent’s end zone.
- After touchdown:
  - Extra point kick: 1 point try (kick from a fixed spot per league rules).
  - Two-point conversion: 2 point try (run/pass attempt from short distance).
- Field goal: worth 3 points if kicked through the uprights during a placekick attempt.
- Safety: offense can concede 2 points if the ball becomes dead in its own end zone due to offensive actions (e.g., tackled in own end zone with possession).

3) Common offensive pre-snap / snap rules
- False start: offensive player moves illegally before the snap; play stops and penalty yards are enforced.
- Illegal formation: not enough players on line of scrimmage or improper alignment; penalty.
- Delay of game: snap not executed before the play clock expires; penalty.

4) Passing rules (high-level)
- A forward pass is allowed once per play and must be thrown from behind the line of scrimmage.
- An “incomplete pass” stops the clock and results in the next down at the previous spot.
- Interceptions: if a defender catches a forward pass in bounds, possession changes and the defender’s team becomes the offense.
- Intentional grounding (conceptual): quarterback throws to avoid a sack without a realistic chance of completion; penalty and loss of down (details vary by situation).

5) Running rules (high-level)
- A runner is down when tackled or when any part of the body (other than hands/feet) touches the ground while contacted, depending on rules context.
- Ball carrier may “slide” to give themselves up; play ends at the slide spot (QB protection concepts apply).

6) Offensive holding (conceptual)
- Blocking must be within legal technique; holding is when an offensive player illegally restricts a defender (e.g., grabbing outside frame to impede).
- Holding often results in a significant loss of yardage and repeats the down (exact enforcement depends on league context).

7) Substitution and personnel
- Offense can substitute, but must allow the defense a reasonable chance to substitute (officials manage this).
- Too many players in the huddle/on the field at the snap is a penalty.

8) Clock management concepts
- Spike: quarterback spikes ball immediately after snap to stop the clock; counts as an incomplete pass and uses a down.
- Kneel: quarterback takes knee to keep clock running; used to close games.
- Timeout usage and “two-minute warning” style stoppages influence play selection.

End of offense notes.
